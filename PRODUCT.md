# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Culturally curious, educated travelers who approach travel as a form of understanding — not leisure consumption. They read long-form journalism, visit museums without a guided tour, and find generic itineraries insulting. Typically 35–60, urban, bilingual (ES/EN), with disposable income but a strong aversion to performative luxury. They are in a research-and-inspiration mode when they arrive: slow readers, deep browsers, resistant to any experience that feels like it's selling them something.

## Product Purpose

Meridiana is a premium cultural travel platform focused on Colombia and Latin America. Its purpose is not to sell tours — it is to make the reader *understand* a place before they decide to go. The site functions as a curated cultural publication that happens to have booking capability. Success looks like a reader spending 8–12 minutes on a tour page, then sending an inquiry — not clicking "Book Now" from a thumbnail grid. Conversion is measured in quality of inquiry, not volume of transactions.

## Brand Personality

Literary. Cinematic. Deliberate.

The brand speaks like a travel writer who has lived in a place, not a copywriter who has researched it. It earns trust through depth and restraint, never through urgency or spectacle. It has a point of view. It trusts the reader's intelligence.

## Anti-references

**Mass tourism platforms** — Airbnb, TripAdvisor, Booking.com, G Adventures, Intrepid Travel: no catalog grids, no review stars, no "from $X/night" cards, no saturated hero sliders, no promotional copy ("Book now and save!"), no feature-benefit bullet lists.

**Superficial luxury** — Black Tomato, Abercrombie & Kent: no dark-gold-on-black "premium" aesthetic, no lifestyle photography of cocktails and infinity pools, no copy that performs exclusivity without substance.

**Generic editorial** — no whitespace minimalism for its own sake, no Kinfolk-style blank typography that says nothing. The editorial register here is dense and grounded, not decorative.

The test: if a user could look at any page and mistake it for a travel agency's website, a booking platform, or a luxury lifestyle brand — it has failed.

## Design Principles

1. **Narrative over interface.** The content is the design. Typography, spacing, and rhythm exist to serve long-form reading, not to signal that design happened. Never decorate what should be read.

2. **Restraint as commitment.** Every element on the page must earn its place through function or meaning. Decorative flourishes, gratuitous animation, and visual noise erode the editorial authority the brand is built on.

3. **Cultural specificity over generic beauty.** The palette, imagery, and tone reference Colombia and Latin America concretely — not as exotic backdrop but as intellectual substance. The design should feel like it could only be for this place.

4. **Depth before breadth.** One tour explained with total clarity is worth more than seven tours in a grid. Progressive disclosure over information dump. Guide the reader into understanding before offering action.

5. **Invisible accessibility.** The reading experience must work for everyone, including those who turn off animation, increase contrast, or use keyboard navigation. Accessibility is built into the editorial design — not bolted on afterward.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.1 AA** minimum for all pages, with editorial legibility as the primary driver (not just checkbox compliance).
- High contrast ratios for body text — target 7:1 on primary reading surfaces, not just the 4.5:1 AA floor.
- All parallax effects, video backgrounds, and scroll-driven animations must have a fully functional, non-animated fallback for `prefers-reduced-motion`. The fallback is not a degraded state — it is a first-class experience.
- No cognitive friction from experimental layout or navigation. Editorial experimentation cannot compromise wayfinding.
- Semantic HTML throughout. Screen readers should be able to navigate the full tour detail page without visual cues.
- Keyboard navigation tested on all interactive elements (Navbar, TourCard, ContactForm, language toggle).
- No information conveyed by color alone.
